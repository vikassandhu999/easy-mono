# Team management and client assignment

When my coaching team changes, I need to invite a coach, see their responsibilities, and change access without leaving clients unattended. Owners use this occasionally on mobile and desktop. The costly mistakes are revoking the wrong invitation, removing an accepted coach through an invitation action, and assigning a client to someone who cannot access the account.

The account menu opens Team. A searchable, status-filtered cursor list shows each name, email, status and server-owned assigned-client count. Invite coach opens a short form with email and names. Successful invitation opens the saved member page, where the owner can verify the email and resend or revoke the pending invitation. An expired invitation remains visible with its server-owned expiry state.

A member page keeps access controls beside an explanation of their effect. Deactivation signs the coach out and transfers their assigned clients to the owner. Reactivation restores access; clients stay with the owner until deliberately reassigned. The owner cannot deactivate themselves. The assigned-client list uses server filtering, search, totals and cursor pagination.

A dense table with row menus would hide access consequences and feel cramped on a phone. A list leading to a focused member page gives each action room for its explanation and confirmation. Client details links to a dedicated Change coach screen: browse active team members, choose one, and confirm the assignment with both client and coach names visible. Current assignment is readable from the API. Each of these routes has a clear return link that preserves the originating task.

Initial failures have retry. Failed pagination and refresh keep loaded rows. Invitation drafts survive failed requests and warn before unsaved departure. Access writes reconcile uncertain results against the member's authoritative status before retrying. Resending is deliberate and explains that it replaces the earlier link. Revoke recovery checks that the member is actually gone; an accepted invitation must not look successfully revoked. Late reads and writes cannot populate another account's cache.

Backend requirements include literal search, tenant-leading keysets, native counts, owner-only management, active-only assignment candidates, and serialization between deactivation and reassignment. Deactivation and invitation acceptance/revocation must remain safe under concurrent requests. The browser never derives persisted totals or eligible candidates by filtering loaded records.

## Invitation recovery audit — 2026-09-15

When a send cannot be confirmed, the owner needs to find the saved invitation without replacing a link the coach may already be opening. The backend reuses an invited member on a second create request, rotates their invitation token and sends another email. Email uniqueness therefore does not make blind retries harmless.

Check Team keeps the invitation details in place and searches the server by the submitted email. Matching coaches retain their current invitation/access status. View coach opens the saved member without claiming another invitation was sent; the originating Team filters survive navigation and reload. Failed lookups offer Retry, and a completed lookup permits returning to retry deliberately. Uncertain details stay locked, pending navigation cannot discard the request, and leaving explains that it does not revoke a sent invitation. Error and retry paths preserve keyboard focus.
